It has been a great time since my last update. I passed all my courses with great results, went to Roskilde Festival, saw Pitbull, Malachi visited Copenhagen where we hung out, DHL and family day at work yesterday.

I feel recharged and ready for my last semester, or technically second last but it feels like the last because of my thesis being the sole thing next year.

My IoT course (conveniently called [Internet of Things](https://kursuskatalog.cbs.dk/2025-2026/KAN-CINTO1014U.aspx)) was quite fun - a bit different from the usual CBS course as we were making an actual IoT product, though we also had a lot about the business landscape IoT products functions in. My group and I made a people counter with a [time of flight sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-of-flight_camera) and Arduino board, so it would increment and decrement the count when people would walk past it. The business case I chose to focus on was airports, where people counters could be used to monitor passenger flows, identify congestion and bottlenecks, and help airports make better decisions around staffing and resource allocation.

The [Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning](https://kursuskatalog.cbs.dk/2025-2026/KAN-CINTO4003U.aspx) course was quite interesting and obviously very relevant these days. I had looked forward to it as I was quite curious about how everything works. It was interesting to learn about convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and how they can be applied in various ways. I especially enjoyed seeing how language models evolved from earlier neural networks to the Transformer architecture, which made models like BERT and GPT possible and eventually led to tools like ChatGPT. We also learned how agents can use LLMs alongside other tools to work through tasks in several steps, and I can see a lot of potential in that.

In [Advanced Strategic Information Management](https://kursuskatalog.cbs.dk/2025-2026/KAN-CINTO1017U.aspx), I wrote about bicycle rental apps in cities. It was interesting to look beyond the app itself and think about the wider ecosystem of riders, bikes, operators, and city infrastructure that has to work together for the service to be useful. The course got me thinking about how a digital platform influences its surroundings, and how decisions about growth and access has an affect. For [Information Economics and Business](https://kursuskatalog.cbs.dk/2025-2026/KAN-CINTO1016U.aspx), I wrote about Netflix. It was a good case for looking at the economics behind a streaming platform: subscription pricing, the value of viewing data, and how that information can shape a business and its competition landscape. I also took [Gamification and Behavior Management](https://kursuskatalog.cbs.dk/2025-2026/KAN-CGMAI3014U.aspx) as a summer course and wrote about Joe & The Juice's loyalty program. The course made me think about how goals, rewards, and feedback can keep customers engaged, but also where the line is between making an experience fun and nudging people to spend more.
